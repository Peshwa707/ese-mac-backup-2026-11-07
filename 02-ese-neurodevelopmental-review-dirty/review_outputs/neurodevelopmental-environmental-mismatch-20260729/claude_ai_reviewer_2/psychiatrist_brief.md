## Literature-supported possibilities

- **H1.1:** Group-level evidence indicates that executive-task and attention differences associated with ADHD are already measurable in childhood, though effects are small and heterogeneous rather than universal. (supported; moderate certainty)
- **H1.2:** ADHD-related symptoms and impairment persist through adolescence in a substantial proportion of affected individuals, with persistence estimates sensitive to informant, comorbidity adjustment and sampling frame. (supported; moderate certainty)
- **H1.3:** ADHD-related symptoms and cross-domain functional impairment frequently persist into adulthood; evidence that laboratory-measured executive dysfunction persists with equal consistency is weaker and mixed. (supported; moderate certainty)
- **H1.4:** Persistent ADHD is associated at group level with concurrent impairment across occupational, educational, social, adaptive and health domains, although comorbidity contributes substantially to this multi-domain burden. (supported; moderate certainty)
- **H2.4:** Achievement or academic success alone is not diagnostic evidence for or against compensation or masking; establishing compensation requires independent measures of effort, cross-setting impairment and developmental history. (supported; moderate certainty)
- **H5.1:** ADHD symptoms are consistently associated with peer and social difficulties and, in large adolescent longitudinal data, precede peer victimisation; the association is presentation-specific and partly mediated by depressive symptoms, and the extracted evidence does not include diagnosed adult samples. (supported; moderate certainty)
- **H5.4:** Co-occurrence across neurodevelopmental and psychiatric conditions is common at group level and shared markers are frequently non-specific, which supports a plural formulation as a working hypothesis; this does not establish which combination applies to an individual, and constructs remain partly dissociable when overlap is controlled. (supported; moderate certainty)
- **H4.2:** CBT and structured behavioral therapy for adult ADHD are associated with moderate improvements in functioning, particularly occupational functioning, with limited and possibly attenuating durability and no reliable quality-of-life gain; coaching specifically lacks comparable adult trial evidence. (supported; moderate certainty)
- **H4.6:** External scaffolds show inconsistent durability, incomplete harms reporting, and documented adverse effects in some digital modalities; evidence for true dependence is not directly measured, and some therapy-based functional gains appear maintained at short follow-up. (supported; moderate certainty)
- **H8.3:** Prescribed stimulants are associated with a low absolute occurrence of psychosis or mania (roughly 0.6% to 3.7% across ADHD cohorts), with higher occurrence for amphetamines than methylphenidate and a dose gradient at higher amphetamine equivalents. Pooled trial data and a national within-person cohort do not show increased manic symptoms or mania-related hospitalization when stimulants are added to mood stabilizers or antipsychotics in bipolar disorder. (partially supported; moderate certainty)
- **H12.1:** Bullying, rejection, shame-related stigma and anxiety are consistently associated with elevated psychological distress and self-reported functional burden across longitudinal and cross-sectional evidence, with heterogeneous magnitude and limited adult occupational data. (supported; moderate certainty)
- **G1:** Baseline neurodevelopmental traits and state or environmental factors demonstrably co-vary, and state factors such as current mood severity, sleep and medication measurably alter apparently trait-level performance; formal statistical interaction among these factors is largely untested in the eligible evidence. (partially supported; moderate certainty)
- **G2:** Context and external support demonstrably alter observable impairment in ADHD and autistic adults, with moderate effect sizes for structured psychological intervention; evidence that internal compensation preserves achievement at a measurable effort cost is absent, so achievement alone cannot establish or exclude masked impairment. (partially supported; moderate certainty)
- **G4:** Retrospective reports in this literature are systematically shaped by informant, measurement method and genetic liability, and self-reported masking and strengths accounts are largely testimonial, so retrospective reconstruction and confirmation bias remain plausible partial contributors to apparent coherence; no eligible record tested them directly. (partially supported; moderate certainty)

## Plausible but weakly evidenced or unresolved components

- **H2.2:** Delayed recognition of neurodevelopmental conditions is well documented and plausibly related to compensatory presentation, but there is no eligible direct evidence in this module that higher intelligence specifically delays recognition of ADHD or autism. (indeterminate; very low certainty)
- **H2.3:** Compensatory processes are plausibly documented at neural and presentation levels, but eligible evidence does not establish that compensation preserves achievement specifically at the cost of increased effort or performance instability. (indeterminate; very low certainty)
- **H11.2:** Motor-coordination difficulties are measurable in community-dwelling midlife adults using adult self-report instruments, but the extracted evidence includes no longitudinal data and cannot establish that such difficulties persist from childhood-onset DCD. (partially supported; very low certainty)
- **H11.3:** Whether motor impairment exceeds what inattention explains is untested in the extracted evidence; the most relevant overlap-controlled meta-analysis shows inattention itself is associated with perceptual-motor weakness, so motor difficulty cannot be presumed independent of attention. (indeterminate; very low certainty)
- **H3.1:** Whether interest specifically moderates attention or task performance in ADHD is untested in this evidence set; a related motivational package improved motor learning in autistic children. (indeterminate; very low certainty)
- **H3.4:** No eligible evidence in this module tests whether urgency or deadlines alter task initiation or performance; only stated preferences for time-related supports were identified. (indeterminate; very low certainty)
- **H10.1:** This evidence set contains no direct test of divergent thinking in ADHD or autism; only indirect neuropharmacological evidence bearing on divergent-task performance was identified. (indeterminate; very low certainty)
- **H10.2:** Creativity is frequently reported as a strength by neurodivergent professionals, but no included study measured real-world creative output comparatively; the association is currently testimonial. (indeterminate; very low certainty)
- **H10.3:** This module identified no eligible evidence on the reliability or clinical meaningfulness of hyperfocus; the construct is untested here rather than validated or refuted. (indeterminate; very low certainty)
- **H4.5:** There is currently no eligible evidence that AI-assisted organization produces durable clinical benefit in adults with ADHD; existing AI mental-health evidence is short-term, indirect, and not focused on executive or organizational functioning. (unsupported; low certainty)
- **H7.3:** Shift work is widely presumed to impair sleep, cognition and mood, but this rapid abstract-level review identified no direct quantified evidence in ADHD or bipolar-spectrum samples, so the claim remains untested here. (indeterminate; very low certainty)
- **H8.4:** This evidence set does not permit a conclusion about whether nicotine produces acute activation or dependence-related state changes. Nicotine or tobacco use disorder co-occurs with stimulant misuse in dual-diagnosis samples and with worse bipolar course indicators, but no eligible record measured nicotine exposure against activation, cognitive or mood outcomes. (indeterminate; very low certainty)
- **H9.2:** An emotional-compensation function for daydreaming is theoretically plausible and consistent with compensatory-use models for related escape behaviours, but no eligible study in this set tested it directly; it should be treated as an untested hypothesis. (indeterminate; very low certainty)
- **H9.3:** Temporal direction between adversity and maladaptive daydreaming has not been established; the available prospective evidence is sparse, addresses adjacent constructs, and is partly confounded by gene-environment correlation. (unsupported; moderate certainty)

## Suggested discriminating questions and assessments

- Developmental interview plus school, training, work, and collateral records predating mood episodes, sleep disruption, substances, and medication changes.
- Structured ADHD, autism, bipolar-spectrum, anxiety, trauma, substance-use, sleep/circadian, and developmental-coordination assessments as clinically appropriate.
- Prospective dated tracking of sleep, mood, substances, medication exposure, speech/activation, unusual salience or certainty, and objective functioning across settings.
- Process measures for compensation: time, effort, support dependence, errors, rebound, recovery cost, and performance when structure changes.

## Immediate safety-relevant considerations

- Prescription amphetamine exposure showed a dose-graded association with incident psychosis or mania (adjusted OR 2.68, 95% CI 1.90-3.77; 5.28-fold above 30 mg dextroamphetamine equivalents) while methylphenidate was null (OR 0.91, 0.54-1.55); this is agent- and dose-specific review evidence, not a treatment recommendation.
- Pooled stimulant-associated occurrence estimates (psychotic disorder 2.29%, bipolar disorder 3.72%) carry I-squared above 95% with no unexposed comparators, so they cannot be converted into individual risk figures; primary authors explicitly disclaim causality.
- Medication-emergent activation is poorly delimited from mood episodes: only 8 of 30 studies defined activation and no validated mania instrument existed. Unusual salience or certainty temporally associated with reduced sleep, mood elevation or substances warrants clinical evaluation, not self-review.
- High-THC cannabis was associated with psychosis risk and with more manic episodes and rapid cycling; the only direct test of an ADHD-specific cannabis-psychosis interaction rested on three studies and was underpowered, so absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.
- The source protocol's prohibition on intentional sleep deprivation, substance exposure or unprescribed medication change is consistent with the evidence: rest-activity instability preceded 6-month mania symptoms in mania-vulnerable youth, and no eligible record establishes safe experimental sleep restriction in bipolar-spectrum illness.
- Cannabis-related cognitive recovery continues for weeks up to about a year after cessation, so short abstinence intervals do not license attributing residual executive difficulty to a stable trait.
- Trait attribution during nominal euthymia can be spurious: dimensional associations with attention performance disappeared after covarying current mania severity, indicating that residual subsyndromal elevation can masquerade as a trait.
- Digital-intervention reviews documented adverse effects including physical discomfort, emotional reactions and gaming addiction in 8 of 26 reviews, with harms inconsistently measured; any self-experiment with scaffolds should change one variable at a time and be reviewed with the treating psychiatrist.
- Longitudinal peer-victimisation reviews link adversity to post-traumatic symptoms, with less consistent self-harm and suicidality findings; a history of rejection and shame is a matter for clinical assessment rather than literature-based inference.
- Nothing in this review constitutes a diagnosis, an individual causal attribution, or a treatment directive; full-text screening, formal risk-of-bias assessment, human adjudication and clinical sign-off remain pending.
