# What we learned from the two evidence reviews

**Audience:** General readers, families, educators, and non-specialist stakeholders
**Review topic:** Neurodevelopmental environmental mismatch
**Reviewers:** One Codex-driven review and one blinded Claude review
**Status:** Plain-language draft. Human clinical review and sign-off are still required.

## Read this first

This review looked for patterns in published research. It did not diagnose anyone, explain one person’s difficulties, or recommend treatment.

The two AI reviewers looked at the same review question and the same set of records. They agreed on many broad patterns, but they also disagreed about important details. Agreement between two AI systems is useful for finding patterns and questions for human review. It is not proof.

### What the confidence words mean

- **Moderate confidence:** The pattern appeared more than once, but important limits remain.
- **Low confidence:** Some evidence points in this direction, but the evidence is limited or indirect.
- **Very low confidence:** The question was barely studied or the available evidence was too weak to answer it.
- **Not supported:** The current evidence does not justify the claim as stated.

## Learning points

### Learning point 1: ADHD-related difficulties can continue into adulthood

Symptoms and difficulties linked with ADHD often continue from childhood into adolescence and adulthood. The exact percentage varies because studies use different definitions, informants, and populations.

**Confidence:** Moderate.
**Important limit:** Persistence of symptoms does not prove that every adult difficulty is caused by ADHD.

### Learning point 2: The effects can show up in several parts of life

Adults with persistent ADHD can experience difficulties at work, in education, in relationships, in daily organization, and in health-related functioning.

**Confidence:** Moderate.
**Important limit:** Other conditions and life circumstances can add to these difficulties.

### Learning point 3: Social rejection and bullying are linked with distress

Peer problems, rejection, stigma, shame, and anxiety are associated with greater emotional distress and a heavier sense of day-to-day burden.

**Confidence:** Moderate.
**Important limit:** The size of the effect varies, and adult workplace evidence was limited.

### Learning point 4: Other factors can look like, or worsen, neurodevelopmental difficulties

Mood episodes, poor sleep, medication effects, substance use, anxiety, depression, trauma, and other health conditions can change attention, energy, behavior, and emotional control.

**Confidence:** Moderate to low.
**Important limit:** The studies do not always measure these factors together, so they cannot reliably show which factor matters most for one person.

### Learning point 5: High achievement does not confirm or rule out ADHD

Doing well in school or work, having high measured intelligence, or appearing organized does not by itself prove that compensation or masking is happening. It also does not rule out meaningful difficulty.

**Confidence:** Low.
**Important limit:** Effort, developmental history, and functioning in more than one setting are needed to understand an individual situation.

### Learning point 6: Neurodevelopmental and mental-health conditions can overlap

ADHD, autism-related traits, anxiety, depression, personality-related difficulties, and other conditions can occur together or produce similar outward problems.

**Confidence:** Moderate to low.
**Important limit:** An overlap in research groups does not identify the correct combination for a particular person.

### Learning point 7: Support and surroundings can change visible functioning

External structure, reminders, workplace changes, educational support, and therapy can improve how someone functions in some situations.

**Confidence:** Low to moderate.
**Important limit:** Improvement in a task or rating does not prove that an underlying difficulty has disappeared. Long-term effects were not well established.

### Learning point 8: The environment can make difficulties easier or harder to see

Highly standardized tests, tightly controlled workplaces, sensory demands, workload, and interpersonal conflict may make difficulties more visible or more disabling.

**Confidence:** Low.
**Important limit:** The evidence was often small, uncontrolled, or based on self-report.

### Learning point 9: Sleep and symptoms affect each other

Poor sleep and irregular sleep schedules are linked with worse attention, executive functioning, mood, and daily functioning. Symptoms can also disturb sleep, so the relationship may work in both directions.

**Confidence:** Low.
**Important limit:** The research does not show that sleep loss alone caused the problems in a particular person.

### Learning point 10: Mood, medication, and substances need to be considered together

Mood state, cannabis, stimulants, nicotine, other medications, sleep, and substance use can change attention, sleep, psychosis risk, or mood stability. These factors should be assessed together rather than one at a time.

**Confidence:** Moderate to low.
**Important limit:** Evidence was uneven, and some specific questions—especially about nicotine and activation—were not directly tested.

### Learning point 11: Many popular explanations were not directly tested

The review did not find enough direct evidence to confirm claims about hyperfocus, creativity as a measurable advantage, interest reliably improving attention, deadlines changing performance, shift work in ADHD or bipolar disorder, or emotional compensation through daydreaming.

**Confidence:** Very low or unanswered.
**What to do with this:** Treat these ideas as hypotheses or personal experiences, not as established scientific facts.

### Learning point 12: AI-assisted organization does not yet have proven lasting clinical benefit

The available evidence did not show that AI-assisted organization produces durable clinical improvement for adults with ADHD.

**Confidence:** Very low to not supported.
**Important limit:** Existing AI mental-health studies were short-term, indirect, or focused on different outcomes.

### Learning point 13: One single explanation is not supported

The evidence did not show that one coherent “adaptive-constraint” model explains all of the findings better than several interacting explanations.

**Confidence:** Very low to moderate against the single-model claim.
**More useful interpretation:** A person’s presentation may reflect several interacting factors, including neurodevelopmental traits, mood, sleep, health, medication, substances, relationships, and environment.

### Learning point 14: The timing between adversity and maladaptive daydreaming is still unclear

The studies did not establish whether adversity causes maladaptive daydreaming, whether daydreaming increases vulnerability to adversity, or whether both are influenced by other factors.

**Confidence:** Not supported as a clear cause-and-effect claim.
**Important limit:** “Not supported” means the current evidence did not establish the claim; it does not prove that the relationship is impossible.

### Learning point 15: The strongest conclusion is about limits, not diagnosis

Research can show group patterns and help rank possible explanations. It cannot determine an individual diagnosis or cause without a proper clinical assessment and relevant personal history.

**Confidence:** High.
**Practical meaning:** Evidence can inform questions for a clinician; it cannot replace the clinician’s assessment.

### Learning point 16: Two AI reviews are useful for finding disagreements, not for replacing people

The two AI reviews agreed on the broad verdict for about 79% of the 66 claims. They still had nine important disagreements, and they agreed about whether evidence directly applied to the case only about 3% of the time.

**Confidence:** High for the process lesson.
**Practical meaning:** AI can organize evidence and highlight questions. Human reviewers must decide what the evidence means, and qualified clinicians must sign off on clinical interpretations.

## What is strongest right now

The most consistent signals were:

- ADHD-related difficulties can persist into adulthood.
- Difficulties can affect several areas of life.
- Social rejection, bullying, stigma, and anxiety are linked with distress.
- External supports may change observable functioning, although durability is uncertain.
- Group research cannot establish an individual diagnosis or cause.

These are **provisional group-level findings**, not conclusions about any one person.

## What remains uncertain

The most important unanswered questions concern:

- how much of a person’s difficulty is due to neurodevelopmental traits versus mood, sleep, medication, substances, or environment;
- whether “masking,” “compensation,” “hyperfocus,” or “creativity” are reliable clinical constructs;
- whether support changes underlying impairment or mainly changes performance in a specific setting;
- the long-term effects of digital tools and AI-assisted organization; and
- which findings apply to a particular person.

## What the review does not authorize

This document does not authorize:

- self-diagnosis;
- a treatment decision;
- a claim that one theory is proven;
- patient-specific advice;
- a public claim that the AI review is equivalent to human review; or
- clinical or official evidence-engine export.

Human Reviewer 2, full-text review, formal risk-of-bias assessment, and clinical sign-off remain required.
